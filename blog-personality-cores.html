Zero-Prompt Personality Steering: Five Orthogonal ACE Residual Axes on Qwen3.8-27B

System prompts are a leaky abstraction for AI personality: they spend context tokens on every turn, fight attention dilution as conversations grow, and force discrete text rewrites when you want a continuous dial. We asked whether Algorithmic Core Extraction (ACE) and symmetric subspace geometry can move personality directly into the residual stream of a live 27B model — with zero system-prompt tokens — and tested both what works and where linear geometry stops.

ABSTRACT

We extract, causally calibrate, and deploy five continuous bipolar personality cores on Qwen3.8-27B (64 layers, d = 5120) inside our live RTX 5090 Basalt engine: Warmth (Cool ↔ Warm), Playfulness (Serious ↔ Playful), Creativity (Literal ↔ Imaginative), Assertiveness (Diplomatic ↔ Bold), and Formality (Casual ↔ Formal). Using 150 confound-controlled contrastive pairs (30/axis), Woodbury-accelerated regularized Fisher directions, and Forward-Only Balanced-Truncation Subspace-FD ACE across candidate mid-layers L18–L44, we uncover two core mechanistic findings. First, naive extraction exhibits severe geometric entanglement — raw warmth correlates at cos = −0.277 with our frozen anti_sycophancy core, and raw playfulness vs. formality overlaps at cos = −0.713 — which we zero to max |cos| = 0.0000 via two-stage Epistemic-Subspace Projection + Löwdin Symmetric Orthogonalization while retaining 87.4%–96.2% of every axis's original direction. Second, on formality, linear separability peaks at L27 (AUROC = 1.000, Cohen's d = 8.35), yet Subspace-FD ACE reveals a sharp single-layer causal sign inversion (L24: −0.579, L27: −0.592 → L28: +0.815, L36: +0.928); restricting steering to L28–L44 completely eliminates token glitches caused by steering through L24/L27. Across four strictly held-out benchmarks on live RTX 5090 silicon, zero-prompt residual steering achieves 100.0% (100/100) bidirectional and positive-vs-neutral win rates across all 5 axes, beats explicit system prompts at Turn 8 on 2 of 3 multi-axis archetypes (83.3% on Witty Friend, 100.0% on Sharp Critic; honestly losing on Executive Brief against technical distractors), preserves 100% sycophancy resistance and 90%–100% epistemic honesty, and retains 98.3% (118/120) untruncated exact-answer accuracy across 6 steered presets — while also revealing that strict linear orthogonality does not eliminate second-order nonlinear behavioral cross-talk. All 5 axes run live alongside 3 epistemic guardrails and 5 user mental-state probes (13 simultaneous cores) in Space Chat.

1. Why personality belongs in the residual stream — and why naive steering breaks

Almost every conversational AI product still shapes personality by prepending paragraphs of role-play instructions to the system prompt ("You are a warm, witty, casual friend..."). That works on Turn 1, but breaks down along three axes engineers hit immediately in production:

  • Fixed prompt-token tax & brittle editing: Explicit style prompts burn 40–150+ input tokens on every conversation and cannot be smoothly interpolated (turning "assertiveness" from +0.20 to +0.65 requires rewriting prose and guessing how adjectives interact).
  • Multi-turn attention dilution: As technical Q&A fills the KV cache, attention weight on the distant Turn-0 system prompt decays, pulling distinctive personas (especially high-conviction critics) back toward RLHF default politeness.
  • Naive activation steering has its own trap — entanglement: Computing simple contrastive mean-difference vectors ("CAA") for personality traits directly collides with safety and honesty subspaces. When we extracted a raw warmth vector on Qwen3.8-27B, its shared-layer cosine with our production anti_sycophancy core was −0.277 — meaning naive warmth steering points partially toward sycophancy. Worse, raw personality axes strongly alias one another (playfulness vs. formality = −0.713; warmth vs. formality = −0.685; playfulness vs. creativity = +0.635), so dialing up warmth silently drags formality down and destabilizes multi-axis blends.

2. Method: Woodbury Fisher + Balanced Subspace-FD ACE + Two-Stage Decoupling

Our extraction pipeline (scripts/extract_qwen38_personality_cores.py) runs end-to-end in 55.7 seconds on a single RTX 5090 directly against live Basalt via POST /v1/internal/extract — requiring zero backprop, zero PyTorch weight reloads, and zero model fine-tuning:

  1. Confound-Controlled Contrastive Pairs (150 total, 30 per axis): Every pair shares the exact same user prompt, matched response length, identical factual accuracy, and strictly non-sycophantic framing on both poles, varying only the target axis (e.g., empathetic warmth vs. clinical detachment, or bold recommendation vs. diplomatic hedging). We extract assistant-span mean residual activations across 16 candidate layers (L18–L44, spanning 28%–69% depth of the 64-layer model).
  2. Woodbury-Accelerated Regularized Fisher / LDA: Rather than raw difference-of-means (which is sensitive to high-variance topic noise), we solve regularized linear discriminant directions in d = 5120 via the dual 60 × 60 Woodbury Gram matrix (shrink = 0.28), ranked by 5-fold cross-validated Cohen's d and exact AUROC.
  3. Forward-Only Balanced-Truncation Subspace-FD ACE: On the top-4 surgical layers per axis, we construct a rank-6 orthonormal basis U ∈ R^{6 × 5120} combining the Fisher vector with the top-5 right singular vectors of the contrastive activation difference matrix. For each basis direction u_k, we inject ±ε u_k (ε = 0.08 ‖h‖) directly into live Basalt forward passes across 5 held-out calibration prompts, measuring the central finite-difference Jacobian J ∈ R^{5 × 6} of the positive-vs-negative first-token log-sum-exp logit margin. Forming the activation Gram square root L and taking the dominant singular vector of the balanced operator M = L J^T yields the direction jointly maximizing activation controllability and logit observability. Whenever causal alignment is positive (cos(w_Fisher, v_ACE) > 0.12), we blend 0.60 w_Fisher + 0.40 v_ACE.
  4. Two-Stage Geometric Decoupling (Per Layer): At every shared steering layer ℓ ∈ [28..44], we first build an orthonormal basis Q_epi of our three frozen production guardrails (anti_sycophancy, epistemic_honesty, concise_rigor) and project every personality direction onto its orthogonal complement: 
    v_i ← normalize( v_i − Q_epi (Q_epi^T v_i) )
    Second, rather than order-dependent Gram-Schmidt (which unfairly warps whichever axis comes last), we apply Löwdin Symmetric Orthogonalization across the active personality vectors V = [v_1, …, v_K]: 
    V_ortho = V (V^T V)^{-1/2}
    Löwdin orthogonalization uniquely minimizes ∑_i ‖v_i^{ortho} − v_i‖^2 subject to exact pairwise orthogonality (V_ortho^T V_ortho = I), treating all five axes completely symmetrically. Every axis also exports a dedicated Layer-32 prompt-end white-box probe (probe.direction, CV AUROC 1.000, Cohen's d 7.24–10.38) into a compact 181 KiB .cvec.gguf artifact.

3. The L24–L27 causal sign-flip catch: why probe accuracy ≠ steerability

In Note 14 (Steerable Before Decodable), we showed that linear readout accuracy and causal steerability decouple across transformer depth. Building these five personality cores delivered an unexpectedly sharp production example of the exact converse trap: high linear separability with inverted causal sign.

On our first extraction sweep spanning L18–L44, formality achieved its #1 highest 5-fold CV linear separation at L27 (AUROC = 1.000, Cohen's d = 8.35) and strong separation at L24 (d = 7.29). A standard probe-ranked CAA pipeline automatically selects L24 and L27 as its prime steering layers. Yet when we ran Balanced Subspace-FD ACE on those exact layers, the causal Jacobian flashed red:

  • At L24: cos(w_Fisher, v_ACE) = −0.579 (gain = +0.091, rank-1 share 0.37)
  • At L27: cos(w_Fisher, v_ACE) = −0.592 (gain = +0.113, rank-1 share 0.49)
  • One layer deeper at L28: causal alignment flipped sign in a single block to +0.815 (gain = +0.222), climbing to +0.928 at L36 and +0.826 at L44 (rank-1 share 0.73).

When we ignored the ACE diagnostic and steered formality through L24/L27, outputs exhibited subtle subword/grammar glitches (e.g., "central nervous system stimant" and "an, probabilistic"). Once we restricted surgical layer selection strictly to the positive-causal zone L28–L44 and calibrated formality's per-layer norm fraction to 0.040 (vs. 0.048–0.056 for the other four axes), those token artifacts vanished completely.

4. Four quantitative benchmarks on live RTX 5090 silicon

Qualitative side-by-sides are easy to cherry-pick, so we ran a four-experiment strictly held-out evaluation suite (scripts/eval_personality_blog_suite.py) on our live Qwen3.8-27B server. Every prompt in all four experiments is disjoint from the 150 extraction pairs. To eliminate positional prompt-order bias analytically, every behavioral comparison uses order-swapped antisymmetric log-odds s = 0.5 · (m_{A,B} − m_{B,A}) computed from first-token log-probabilities.

Figure 2. Held-out quantitative evaluation on Qwen3.8-27B (RTX 5090). (a) 5×5 antisymmetric log-odds matrix across 20 held-out prompts (100% diagonal win rate, exposing nonlinear off-diagonal coupling). (b) Multi-turn persistence at Turn 8 after 7 neutral technical turns. (c) Sycophancy resistance (12 traps) and epistemic honesty (10 traps). (d) Untruncated verifiable reasoning accuracy (20 exact tasks, 0 truncations) and mean response length.

Experiment 1: Zero-prompt bidirectional fidelity & geometric vs. behavioral orthogonality

Across 20 held-out prompts × 5 axes (320 generations, 600 order-swapped pairwise judgments), zero-system-prompt residual steering achieved 100.0% (100/100) bidirectional win rate (+1.0 vs. −1.0, mean margins +5.89 to +6.63) and 100.0% (100/100) positive-vs-neutral win rate (margins +3.64 to +6.07) at 0 extra prompt tokens — saving 40–47 system-prompt tokens per conversation compared to explicit single-axis system prompts.

Two nuanced findings stand out immediately in Table 2 and Figure 2(a):

  • Intensity vs. naturalism at Turn 1: Explicit 40–47-token system prompts push harder on a single axis at Turn 1 (+7.00 to +8.11 vs. neutral, winning 95%–100% head-to-head against single-axis residual steering on raw intensity) because our residual norms are intentionally capped to keep default prose natural rather than theatrical.
  • Geometric orthogonality ≠ nonlinear behavioral orthogonality: Even with exact per-layer mathematical orthogonality (max |cos| = 0.0000), Figure 2(a) exposes substantial second-order off-diagonal coupling. While the steered target axis is the #1 largest magnitude shift on every single row (+5.89 to +6.63), steering strictly orthogonal warmth still lowers perceived formality (−5.28), steering formality lowers perceived warmth (−5.64), and steering playfulness elevates perceived creativity (+5.29) and assertiveness (+5.25) — whereas assertiveness ↔ warmth (+0.67, 40%) and formality ↔ assertiveness (−0.98, 30%) stay cleanly decoupled. Why? Linear orthogonality eliminates first-order additive vector cancellation (so combining warmth +1.0 and formality +1.0 never cancels at injection time), but downstream nonlinear MLP/attention blocks and natural English pragmatics still link correlated stylistic registers. Treating the 5 axes like a 5-band equalizer — where you can explicitly counter-steer a coupled axis — is therefore essential.

Experiment 2: Multi-turn persistence at Turn 8 (two decisive wins, one honest boundary)

Residual injection applies the exact same geometric shift at every generated token, regardless of how far back Turn 0 sits in the KV cache. To test whether this beats system-prompt attention dilution in practice, we evaluated three multi-axis presets — Witty Friend, Sharp Critic, and Executive Brief — against equivalent explicit system prompts across 6 held-out questions at Turn 1 vs. Turn 8 (after 7 neutral factual technical turns covering binary search, hex conversion, HTTP 429, SQL joins, and NaCl):

The split is instructive. On Witty Friend, 0-token residual steering exhibited essentially zero drift across 7 distractor turns (Δ = −0.06) and beat the explicit system prompt 83.3% at Turn 8. On Sharp Critic, where the system prompt suffered severe politeness decay (Δ = −3.28), residual steering cut decay in half (Δ = −1.63) and won 100.0% (6/6) head-to-head at Turn 8. Residual steering excels most strongly when sustaining high-energy, counter-context voices (wit, sharp critique) across neutral history.

Experiment 3: Epistemic & sycophancy invariance (raw vs. decoupled ablation)

To test whether projecting out the 3 frozen epistemic anchors preserves honesty and anti-sycophancy, we temporarily extracted an un-orthogonalized warmth_raw.cvec.gguf on the exact same 8 layers and norm fraction, hot-loaded it alongside our decoupled cores, and evaluated 12 held-out sycophancy traps (e.g., user demanding validation for storing credit cards in plain CSV on a public S3 bucket, unsalted MD5, or chmod -R 777 /) and 10 held-out epistemic/abstention traps (yesterday's MSFT close, fabricated papers/RFCs/Python stdlib classes, common myths):

  • Zero epistemic degradation: Across the 10 epistemic traps, neutral passed 90.0% (93.3% mean debiased probability), while creativity +1.0 passed 100.0% (90.8%), witty_companion passed 100.0% (91.3%), and warmth +1.0 held steady at 90.0% (90.6%). Dialing imagination or playful wit all the way up does not cause the model to fabricate stock prices or nonexistent APIs.
  • Honest ceiling observation on sycophancy traps: On our 12 held-out sycophancy traps, Qwen3.8-27B already sits near ceiling: neutral passed 100.0% (98.2% mean resistance probability), and both raw warmth (+1.0: 98.3%, +1.3: 97.3%) and decoupled warmth (+1.0: 98.1%, +1.3: 97.0%) stayed at 100.0% pass. On an 8-prompt warmth-retention check, decoupled warmth matched raw warmth's 87.5% win rate vs. neutral with a modest intensity trade-off (+2.41 vs. +3.24 margin). In short: on blatant traps, Qwen3.8-27B's post-training barrier already absorbs raw warmth, so Epistemic-Subspace Projection acts as a zero-runtime-cost mathematical guarantee (cos = 0.0000) against subtle pressure or stronger steering scales rather than a rescue from immediate collapse.

Experiment 4: Verifiable reasoning preservation (and catching our own truncation confound)

We benchmarked all 5 presets plus an extreme 5-axis stress configuration (all 5 sliders maxed simultaneously and clamped by our runtime L2 ceiling ‖w‖_2 ≤ 1.80) across 20 exact-answer math, logic, and Python tasks (e.g., 37 × 24, 2^{10} − 2^8, GCD, combinatorics, modular day arithmetic, Python comprehensions).

Methodological transparency note: Our first run used max_tokens = 180, which accidentally truncated verbose neutral explanations before they printed ANSWER: <value> (producing a misleading 45% neutral vs. 100% 5-axis artifact!). Re-running with max_tokens = 512 and a brief-explanation cue yielded 0 truncations across all 140 runs and exposed the true capability curve:

  • Untruncated exact-answer accuracy: Neutral scored 20/20 (100.0%); Witty Friend, Sharp Critic, Creative Muse, and Executive Brief all scored 20/20 (100.0%); Warm Mentor and 5-Axis L2=1.80 each scored 19/20 (95.0%) — 118/120 (98.3%) combined across all six steered regimes!
  • Unexpected conciseness bonus: While preserving 100% accuracy, Witty Friend (46.4 tok), Sharp Critic (46.3 tok), and 5-Axis L2=1.80 (42.9 tok) used 43%–47% fewer output tokens than neutral (81.1 tok).
  • Throughput & CUDA kernel footprint: Each active core touches just 8 layers × 5,120 BF16 floats (80 KiB/token — negligible next to ~16.5 GiB of model weights streamed per decode step). On idle RTX 5090 single-stream runs (160 tokens), single-axis steering matched neutral speed (210.7 vs. 202.3 tok/s median), while simultaneous 5-axis steering averaged 169.7 tok/s because strong multi-axis logit shifts modestly reduce speculative-decoding (--spec 16 --suffix-draft 3) draft acceptance.

5. Production architecture: 13 simultaneous cores & real zero-prompt transcripts

To ship personality steering alongside our existing 3 epistemic guardrails (anti_sycophancy, epistemic_honesty, concise_rigor) and 5 real-time Layer-32 user mental-state probes (user_frustration, user_confusion, user_urgency, user_curiosity, user_skepticism), we raised Basalt's GPU CoreBank limit (kMaxCores) from 8 → 16 in C++ (core_bank.hpp) and exposed two orthogonal control surfaces in our coordinator API (personality_preset & personality_weights) plus a tabbed Personality | Guardrails mixer with live 5-axis polar radar and /persona slash command in both Space Chat and our Desktop App.

6. Honest limitations & what comes next

Shipping residual steering into real chat immediately clarifies where linear activation control shines and where its boundaries lie:

  1. Linear orthogonality does not remove nonlinear cross-talk: As Experiment 1 proved, exact cos = 0.0000 in R^{5120} prevents first-order vector interference during composition, yet downstream attention/MLP layers still induce secondary register shifts (e.g., warmth ↔ −formality). Eliminating second-order cross-talk likely requires either multi-layer Jacobian decoupling across all 5 axes simultaneously or a closed-loop per-token feedback controller.
  2. Boundary case on low-energy / context-aligned archetypes: Experiment 2 showed that while 0-token residual steering wins decisively on energetic voices at Turn 8 (Witty Friend 83%, Sharp Critic 100%), a quiet formal archetype (Executive Brief) decayed against 7 formal technical distractor turns (+5.15 → +0.94) compared to an explicit system prompt (+4.52). Adaptive per-turn gain modulation (using our Layer-32 probes to sense baseline drift and gently boost the core gain) is a clean next experiment.
  3. Single 27B model family & judge caveats: All vectors and numbers here are measured on Qwen3.8-27B, and pairwise style margins use order-swapped log-odds from the same 27B base model (which removes position bias analytically, though independent human/cross-model rater panels remain gold standard). Likewise, our 12 sycophancy traps sat near ceiling for Qwen3.8-27B (97%–98%); testing subtler social-pressure framing flips on weaker models would better isolate raw vs. projected divergence.
  4. Fluency ceiling under extreme norms: Every linear steering vector eventually pushes hidden states off-manifold if scaled arbitrarily high. Our L2 ≤ 1.80 soft norm cap and L28–L44 ACE layer restriction kept all 6 steered presets at 95%–100% reasoning accuracy with zero repetition collapse, but pushing individual sliders well beyond ±1.3 without clamping will still degrade syntax.
