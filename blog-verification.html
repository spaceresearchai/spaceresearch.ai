Knowledge Boundary Fingerprinting: Verifying Open Models Without Slow Proofs

Open compute networks face a subtle economic attack: advertising a flagship 27B or 70B model while quietly serving a cheap 7B substitute. Here is how we verify genuine open weights across heterogeneous NVIDIA and Apple Silicon hardware with zero latency penalty on user traffic.

ABSTRACT

Exact cryptographic execution proofs for large transformers impose 100×–10,000× compute overhead, making interactive streaming impossible. Conversely, heterogeneous GPUs (NVIDIA CUDA vs. Apple Silicon Metal/MLX) do not even produce bit-identical floating-point outputs for the exact same weights. In SIP-0001, we replace heavy proving with a layered optimistic verification stack anchored by Tier-0 Knowledge Boundary Fingerprinting (KBF), Ed25519 signed execution receipts, hidden deterministic canaries, and hardware-tolerant statistical spot-checks.

1. Why bit-exact verification fails in the real world

Pulling everyday hardware together means combining NVIDIA RTX workstations, Apple Silicon Macs running MLX, and standard Ollama or llama.cpp backends. Even when two honest machines run the exact same open checkpoint, minor floating-point accumulation differences across CUDA and Metal guarantee that naive bit-for-bit output hashing produces false alarms.

Meanwhile, heavy cryptographic execution proving adds orders of magnitude of compute overhead — destroying the entire speed and cost advantage of open local hardware. We need verification that is hardware-tolerant for honest nodes yet brutally sensitive to model downgrades.

2. Tier-0 Knowledge Boundary Fingerprinting (KBF)

Every model scale leaves a sharp signature near its knowledge and reasoning boundary: specific factual, numerical, and multi-step problems that a genuine 27B checkpoint solves reliably, while a downgraded 7B or heavily degraded substitute fails consistently.

Our Tier-0 KBF system curates open, versioned Verification Packs hashed with canonical SHA-256 digests. Background audits periodically challenge active stations across these boundary probes and compute binomial confidence against the genuine baseline. Because probes run asynchronously out-of-band, KBF adds zero milliseconds of latency to interactive user prompts and works uniformly across native runtimes as well as Ollama, llama.cpp, vLLM, and MLX adapters.

4. Cross-hardware statistical invariants

On native stations, async spot-checks compare top-k next-token probability distributions via KL divergence (D_KL ≤ 0.15) and low-dimensional state cosine similarity (cos ≥ 0.90). Honest NVIDIA CUDA and Apple Metal executions consistently sit well inside those bounds (D_KL ≪ 0.05, cos > 0.98), whereas swapped or corrupted weights diverge sharply and trigger automatic removal from routing.
