Silent Cancellation and Backward Gradient Corruption in Low-Precision KL Divergence: Analysis and Fused Triton Kernels

Marcio Diaz · Space AI
marcio.diaz@gmail.com · https://spaceresearch.ai/papers/pytorch-kl-bregman-stability.html
ABSTRACT

Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence regularization is foundational to modern reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), and logit distillation over large vocabularies (V = 32,000-151,936). Yet standard PyTorch F.kl_div evaluates sum_i p_i (log p_i - log q_i), suffering catastrophic cancellation whenever policy and reference distributions lie within typical trust-region proximity. Extending prior forward-pass observations across 36 regimes (forward and reverse KL; FP16, BF16, FP32; eps in [1e-4, 1e-1]), we show this pathology corrupts the backward pass directly: naive F.kl_div produces negative KL on up to 50.4% of inputs and 39.2% reverse-KL gradient sign flips (BF16, eps=1e-2); the common clamp(min=0) patch eliminates negative scalars only by inducing up to 50.4% silent zero-gradient dead-zones (61.8% sign flips); and naive BF16 Schulman k3 (exp(d)-1-d) suffers 100% premature zeroing for eps <= 1e-2. We derive an overflow-free two-branch 6th-order Bregman-Taylor formulation with cancellation-free analytic gradients and implement a fused Blackwell Triton forward/backward kernel that streams online logsumexp in FP32 registers without materializing (N, V) FP32 intermediates. On an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090, our kernel achieves 0.0% negative KL across all 36 regimes (Wilcoxon p=1.45e-11), cuts FP32 (eps=1e-4) relative error from 21.1 to 1.42e-04 (149,111x reduction), runs 6.52x faster with 80.0% less peak VRAM than FP32-upcast F.kl_div at Qwen3 V=151,936 (30.4x faster than eager Bregman-Taylor), and matches the FP32 oracle exactly in 3-seed BF16 RLHF policy optimization (V=32,000) where clamped F.kl_div collapses into negative true objective (-0.00162 vs. +0.00164) with 99.4% dead-KL steps.

1. Introduction

Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence penalties anchor nearly every post-training pipeline for large language models: proximal trust regions in RLHF [7] and GRPO [2], on-policy distillation, and speculative decoding verification. By Gibbs' inequality, DKL(p || q) ≥ 0 for all probability distributions, vanishing if and only if p = q. Nevertheless, recent numerical audits [8] highlighted that PyTorch's standard F.kl_div routinely returns strictly negative values in floating-point arithmetic whenever p and q are close.

Prior discussions largely treated negative KL as a benign forward-pass logging artifact fixable by clamp(min=0) or FP32 upcasting. In this paper, we show that both remedies fail under frontier LLM post-training conditions (BF16/FP16 activations and vocabularies V ∈ [32k, 152k]). Specifically, we make four contributions:

  • Backward-Pass Gradient Audit (Forward & Reverse KL): Across 36 regimes (2 KL directions × 3 dtypes × 6 perturbation scales), we prove that naive F.kl_div induces up to 39.1% reverse-KL gradient sign flips (BF16, ε=10−2), while clamp(min=0) creates silent zero-gradient dead-zones on up to 50.4% of tokens and worsens sign flips to 61.8%.
  • Overflow-Free Bregman-Taylor Formulation & Cancellation-Free Analytic Gradients: We combine a 6th-order Horner Taylor polynomial for |d| < 0.05 with the overflow-immune identity max(q − p − p·d, 0) for |d| ≥ 0.05, eliminating both small-|d| premature zeroing (where BF16 Schulman k3 exhibits 100% relative error) and large-|d| exp(d) overflow.
  • Fused Blackwell Triton Forward/Backward Kernel: Our 3-pass tiled SRAM kernel computes online FP32 logsumexp, exact Bregman-Taylor KL, and analytic logit gradients directly from raw BF16/FP16 logits without materializing (N, V) FP32 HBM tensors, achieving 6.52× speedup and 80.0% VRAM reduction vs. FP32-upcast at Qwen3 V=151,936 (30.4× faster than eager Bregman-Taylor).
  • End-to-End RLHF Verification: In 3-seed BF16 KL-regularized policy optimization (V=32,000), our kernel matches the certified FP32 oracle to 5 decimal places (+0.00164), whereas clamped F.kl_div collapses into negative objective (-0.00162) with 99.4% dead-KL steps.

2. Related Work

Low-Variance & Nonnegative KL Estimators. Schulman [1] introduced the nonnegative control-variate estimator k3 = exp(d) − 1 − d for Monte Carlo RLHF KL estimation, now widely used in GRPO [2]. However, when evaluated over full categorical vocabularies in BF16/FP16, k3 suffers both catastrophic cancellation as d → 0 (rounding exp(d) − 1 to d) and exponent overflow when d > 11.1 in FP16. Recent work [8] documented negative KL values in PyTorch F.kl_div; we extend this analysis to reverse-KL backward gradients, clamping failure modes, and high-vocabulary systems execution.

IO-Aware Fused Kernels for LLM Training. FlashAttention [5] and Liger Kernel [4] demonstrated that fusing vocabulary-scale reductions in Triton [3] removes massive HBM bottlenecks from large-V linear/cross-entropy heads. Our kernel applies the same streaming online-normalizer principle specifically to numerically exact forward and reverse Bregman-Taylor KL divergence with fused analytic backward passes, grounded in classical floating-point error analysis [6].

3. Mathematical Analysis & Fused Triton Kernel

Let zp, zq ∈ RV denote reference and policy logits with normalized log-probabilities lp = zp − logsumexp(zp), lq = zq − logsumexp(zq), p = exp(lp), q = exp(lq), and log-ratio di = lq,i − lp,i. Standard F.kl_div evaluates Σi pi (−di), summing O(ε) terms of opposite sign whose first-order contributions cancel (Σ pi di = −DKL = O(ε2)). Whenever ε2 ≲ umach (umach ≈ 7.8×10−3 in BF16, 9.8×10−4 in FP16, 1.2×10−7 in FP32), rounding error dominates the O(ε2) signal, flipping the sign of DKL with ~50% probability.

Why Reverse-KL Gradients Corrupt Directly. For RLHF/GRPO reverse KL DKL(q || p), the exact logit gradient is ∂DKL(q||p)/∂zq,i = qi((lq,i − lp,i) − DKL(q||p)). Not only does clamping DKL at zero kill the entire gradient vector on every negative-KL token, but even unclamped low-precision evaluation injects the corrupted scalar DKL(q||p) directly into every vocabulary entry's gradient.

Overflow-Free Bregman-Taylor Identity. Adding the zero-sum term Σi (qi − pi) = 0 yields the strictly nonnegative Bregman generator φ(d) = ed − 1 − d ≥ 0. To eliminate BOTH small-|d| cancellation and large-|d| exponential overflow (since pi edi = qi ≤ 1), we split at τ = 0.05:

ti(di) = pi · di2 (1/2 + di/6 + di2/24 + di3/120 + di4/720)    if |di| < τ(1)
ti(di) = max(qi − pi − pi di, 0)    if |di| ≥ τ(2)
gfwd,i = pi(di + di2/2 + … + di5/120) if |di| < τ else (qi − pi) ;   grev,i = qi((lq,i − lp,i) − DKLexact)(3)

At |d| = 0.05, the 7th-order Taylor remainder is 0.057/5040 ≈ 1.5×10−13 (exact to FP32 machine precision), while for |d| ≥ 0.05, qi − pi − pi di never calls exp(di) and cannot overflow. Our Triton kernel executes three tiled SRAM passes (BLOCK_V = 4096) per token row: (i) streaming online FP32 logsumexp for zp and zq, (ii) FP32 accumulation of Eqs. (1)-(2), and (iii) direct HBM write of analytic logit gradients Eq. (3) with zero (N, V) FP32 temporaries.

4. Experimental Setup

All experiments run on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 using PyTorch 2.11.0+cu130 and Triton 3.6.0. We evaluate five methods under identical inputs: (1) native PyTorch F.kl_div, (2) F.kl_div.clamp(min=0), (3) native Schulman k3 (exp(d)−1−d), (4) FP32-upcast F.kl_div, and (5) our Fused Triton Bregman-Taylor kernel. Four testbeds cover accuracy, ablations, systems scaling, and training:

  • Numerical Forward & Backward Audit: N = 2,048 categorical distributions over V = 4,096, base logits N(0, 1.52), perturbation scales ε ∈ {10−4, 3×10−4, 10−3, 3×10−3, 10−2, 10−1}, across FP16, BF16, FP32 and both Forward and Reverse KL (36 total regimes) compared against a certified FP64 Bregman oracle.
  • Threshold Ablation: Sweeping τ ∈ {0, 10−3, 10−2, 0.05, 0.2} in FP32 at ε = 10−4.
  • Frontier Vocabulary Scaling: CUDA-event forward+backward latency (20 timed runs after 5 warmups) and peak extra VRAM across V ∈ {256, 32,000 (Llama-2), 128,256 (Llama-3), 151,936 (Qwen3)} in BF16.
  • Downstream RLHF/GRPO Policy Optimization: 3-seed BF16 training of a linear policy head (V = 32,000, D = 256, β = 2.0, 120 Adam steps) initialized near pref (σ = 10−3), evaluated on certified FP64 true objective J*(q) = Eq[r] − β DKL(q || pref).

6. Ablation Study & Numerical Diagnostics

Table 4 isolates the necessity of our 6th-order Taylor branch inside the fused FP32 Triton kernel. Setting τ = 0 (evaluating max(q − p − p·d, 0) everywhere without the Taylor switch) collapses FP32 accuracy back to 20.46 median relative error (100% of samples exceeding 1% error), confirming that catastrophic cancellation occurs inside FP32 registers as well. Enabling the Taylor branch for any τ ∈ [10−3, 0.2] restores median relative error to 1.42e-04 (0.0% error ≥ 1%) with exact gradient cosine 1.0000 and zero measurable latency overhead.

7. Honest Limitations & Scope

  • Irreducible Input Quantization Floor: When raw input logits zp, zq are already rounded to BF16 prior to the kernel call and |zq − zp| < 0.5 ULPBF16 (≈ 0.004), distinct latent logits round to identical BF16 values. No post-quantization kernel can recover sub-ULP input loss (hence BF16 gradient cosine is bounded by ~0.71 at ε = 10−2 and ~0.23 at ε = 10−3 for both Triton and FP32-upcast); however, keeping policy head matmul outputs in FP32 or passing unrounded logits eliminates even this residual floor.
  • Full-Vocabulary vs. Sampled-Token KL: Our kernel computes exact full-vocabulary categorical KL (as used in on-policy distillation and dense-logit RLHF). Token-sampled Monte Carlo GRPO estimators that only materialize log-probabilities of generated tokens have O(1) memory per token and benefit primarily from Eq. (1) scalar stabilization rather than tiled V-reduction.
  • Continuous Distributions: Closed-form Bregman-Taylor stabilization for continuous exponential families (e.g., Gaussian VAEs) requires family-specific cancellation-free identities.
