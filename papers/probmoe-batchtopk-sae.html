Margin-Gated Probabilistic Surrogate Gradients for Overcomplete Sparse Autoencoders

Marcio Diaz (marcio.diaz@gmail.com) · Space AI (spaceresearch.ai) · Version 4.0 · Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090

Download PDF Preprint (v4.0) ↓ Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24522712 ↗ Raw GPU Metrics JSON
ABSTRACT

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) trained with hard TopK or BatchTopK activations rely on straight-through estimators (STE) that route zero gradient to inactive dictionary atoms, driving severe dead-latent collapse at high expansion factors (up to 78.6% dead atoms at 4x overcompleteness). Inspired by a CoreRank cross-domain bridge linking ProbMoE's differentiable marginal subset routing to BatchTopK SAEs, we first prove formally why naive full-backward probabilistic replacement (ProbBatchTopK v1) degrades reconstruction fidelity: multiplying active top-k gradients by sigmoid derivatives distorts feature magnitudes. We introduce MarginProb-BatchTopK, which preserves exact identity STE gradients on the active top-k set while applying scale-invariant calibrated marginal inclusion probabilities p_ij = sigmoid((z_ij - tau)/(tau_rel * std(z))) exclusively to near-margin inactive latents. We benchmark 9 L0-matched SAE architectures at 4x (d_sae=8,192) and 8x (d_sae=16,384) overcompleteness across two distinct open-weight LLM families (Qwen3-1.7B L13 and SmolLM2-1.7B L12; 336,474 real tokens with strict paper-level train/test splits), three sparsity levels (k in {16, 32, 64}), and 3 random seeds. Across all 18 paired 4x runs, active-set gating completely eliminates v1's FVU penalty (delta FVU = -0.01355, 95% CI [-0.01512, -0.01199], paired t p=4.24e-12). Against pure-MSE BatchTopK-STE, MarginProb-BatchTopK simultaneously significantly reduces FVU (-0.00502, p=1.65e-03) and slashes dead latents by -18.95 percentage points (p=4.84e-08) with ZERO auxiliary loss. Splicing trained k=32 SAEs directly back into the live transformer residual streams confirms causal fidelity: MarginProb-BatchTopK recovers 98.6% CE loss on Qwen3-1.7B (KL=0.2910 vs. 97.7% / KL=0.3523 for ungated v1 and 98.9% for BatchTopK-STE) and 99.4% on SmolLM2-1.7B (KL=0.2040 vs. 99.2% BatchTopK-STE). Scaling to 8x overcompleteness (d_sae=16,384 on Qwen3-1.7B L13, k=32), MarginProb-BatchTopK cuts dead latents from 77.4% (BatchTopK-STE) and 58.5% (BatchTopK+AuxK) down to 52.9% while achieving lower FVU (0.2586 vs. 0.2663). Sweeping lambda in [0, 0.60] smoothly controls dead latents from 54.9% down to 1.18% with an FVU optimum at lambda=0.05 (0.2608).

1. Introduction

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) have emerged as a central tool for decomposing polysemantic transformer residual streams into interpretable sparse features [1, 7]. Recent architectures replaced L1-penalized ReLU SAEs [1] with hard sparsity operators — TopK [2], BatchTopK [3], and JumpReLU [4] — to eliminate L1 shrinkage bias. Yet hard TopK/BatchTopK gating relies on a straight-through estimator (STE) [6] whose backward pass assigns identically zero gradient to every unselected feature. At overcomplete dictionary widths (4× or wider), this zero-gradient dead-zone causes substantial dictionary capacity to collapse into dead latents unless explicitly rescued via auxiliary losses (AuxK [2, 3]), and contributes to seed-to-seed feature instability [5].

Motivated by a CoreRank cross-domain mechanism bridge connecting Mixture-of-Experts probabilistic subset routing (ProbMoE [8]) to BatchTopK SAEs [3], we investigate whether calibrated marginal selection probabilities can provide principled smooth gradients through BatchTopK routing. In an initial exploratory study (v1), replacing the entire BatchTopK backward pass with a soft probabilistic surrogate reduced dead latents but degraded reconstruction FVU. In Version 2.0, we diagnose the exact root cause — active-set gradient distortion — and introduce MarginProb-BatchTopK, evaluated at 4× overcompleteness (dsae = 8,192) across two distinct LLM families (Qwen3-1.7B and SmolLM2-1.7B), 9 SAE methods, 3 sparsity budgets, 3 random seeds, downstream semantic probe recovery, and 1-to-1 Hungarian cross-seed dictionary matching.

2. Related Work

Sparse Autoencoder Architectures & Dead-Latent Mitigation. Bricken et al. [1] and Cunningham et al. [7] popularized L1-regularized ReLU SAEs for LLM interpretability. Gao et al. [2] introduced TopK SAEs with AuxK — an auxiliary loss reconstructing the residual error using top-kaux dead latents — directly reducing dead features and improving the L0-MSE frontier. Bussmann et al. [3] relaxed per-sample TopK to BatchTopK, allowing variable L0 per token while matching a batch sparsity budget. Rajamanoharan et al. [4] proposed JumpReLU SAEs with learned thresholds and rectangular-kernel STE. Paulo & Belrose [5] revealed that SAEs trained on identical data with different seeds learn substantially divergent feature sets.

Differentiable Top-k & Probabilistic Routing. Straight-through estimators [6] leave unselected coordinates without gradient signal. ProbMoE [8] demonstrated that solving for a scalar threshold τ calibrating marginal Bernoulli selection probabilities pj = σ((zj − τ)/T) to match a routing budget yields unbiased, smooth gradients across the selection boundary. We adapt this mechanism to overcomplete BatchTopK SAEs and isolate active-set gradient gating as the decisive factor governing reconstruction fidelity.

3. Diagnosis & MarginProb-BatchTopK Formulation

Let x ∈ Rd be a residual-stream activation, pre-activation z = (x − bdec)Wenc + benc ∈ RM, and hard BatchTopK binary mask M ∈ {0, 1}B×M selecting the B·k largest positive entries across a batch of size B. In naive full-backward replacement (v1 ProbBatchTopK), aijsoft = pij · softplus(zij) replaced the gradient even on active coordinates (Mij = 1), multiplying active gradients by pij σ(βzij) + pij(1 − pij) softplus(zij) / T. Near the boundary, this amplifies gradients 2-5× unpredictably and biases active feature magnitudes, degrading FVU.

MarginProb-BatchTopK eliminates active-set distortion cleanly by partitioning the backward pass strictly on M:

τ : Σi,j σ((zij − τ) / T) = B · k ,   T = τrel(t) · std(z)(1)
ãij = aijhard + λt (1 − Mij) [ pij softplusβ(zij) − sg(pij softplusβ(zij)) ](2)
∂ãij / ∂zij = 1   if Mij=1 ;    λt ∂[pij softplusβ(zij)] / ∂zij   if Mij=0(3)

Here Eq. (1) is solved on GPU via 12-iteration vectorized bisection (<0.2 ms) using scale-invariant temperature T = τrel(t)·std(z) annealed from 1.2τrel to 0.5τrel, and λt follows a cosine schedule. Active features receive exact identity STE gradients (zero magnitude distortion), while inactive latents near the threshold τ receive residual-aligned gradients proportional to −(ei · Wdec,j) · pij, gently pulling useful near-margin features across the activation boundary.

Theorem 1 (Active-Set Unbiasedness & Residual-Aligned Revival). Let ei = xi − x̂i be the reconstruction residual. (i) For every active coordinate (Mij = 1, zij > 0), ∂ãij/∂zij = 1 identically, whereas ungated v1 multiplies active gradients by γij = pijσ(βzij) + T−1pij(1 − pij)softplusβ(zij) ≠ 1, distorting optimal active magnitudes. (ii) For every inactive coordinate (Mij = 0), ∂ãij/∂zij = λt γij > 0 strictly, so under MSE loss ½||ei||2 the encoder update −∇zij L = λt γij (eiT Wdec,j) strictly increases zij whenever atom j positively correlates with the unexplained residual (eiT Wdec,j > 0), with weight concentrated exponentially around the BatchTopK boundary |zij − τ| = O(T).

4. Experimental Setup

Leak-Free Multi-Model Activation Corpora. We physically prune two distinct 1.7B open-weight transformers (both din = 2,048) to mid-depth — Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B (L13/28, 165,417 tokens) and HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-1.7B (L12/24, 171,057 tokens) — across 1,100 scientific abstracts from 8 distinct ML/AI domains. Crucially, we enforce a strict paper-level 80/20 train/test split (zero shared documents between train and test tokens) and fit activation centering/RMS normalization solely on training tokens.

Architectures, Baselines & Metrics. All 9 methods train 4× overcomplete SAEs (dsae = 8,192) for 900 Adam steps (batch size 512, lr = 10−3, unit-norm decoder projection) across k ∈ {16, 32, 64} and 3 seeds (162 main runs + 24 ablation runs on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090). ReLU-L1 and JumpReLU use dual-ascent penalty adaptation to match target L0 fairly; AuxK uses kaux = 256, αaux = 1/32, dead window = 100 steps. We measure held-out FVU, dead-latent %, normalized dictionary utilization entropy H/log(dsae), downstream 8-class paper-category linear probe recovery (% of original activation probe accuracy), and exact 1-to-1 Hungarian bipartite cross-seed cosine similarity.

5. Main Empirical Results

Active-Set Gating Eliminates v1's FVU Penalty (Tables 1-2 & Fig. 1a-b). Across all 18 paired runs (2 models × 3 sparsity levels × 3 seeds), MarginProb-BatchTopK reduces held-out FVU relative to naive ungated v1 ProbBatchTopK by -0.01355 (95% CI [-0.01512, -0.01199], paired t p = 4.24e-12, Wilcoxon p = 1.96e-04), confirming our diagnosis that active-set gradient distortion caused v1's reconstruction deficit.

Pareto Dominance over BatchTopK-STE & Crossover vs. AuxK / TopK. Against pure-MSE BatchTopK-STE across all 18 paired runs, MarginProb-BatchTopK simultaneously achieves statistically significant lower FVU (mean Δ = -0.00502, p = 1.65e-03, Wilcoxon p = 4.75e-03) AND -18.95 percentage points fewer dead latents (p = 4.84e-08) using pure MSE alone. Remarkably, at k = 32 it outperforms even published SOTA BatchTopK+AuxK in FVU on both Qwen3-1.7B (0.2609 vs. 0.2616, with 20.1% vs. 31.2% dead latents) and SmolLM2-1.7B (0.2484 vs. 0.2490, achieving the highest downstream 8-class probe recovery of all 9 methods at 101.8%). At k = 64, MarginProb slashes dead latents 8× below BatchTopK+AuxK on Qwen3 (1.53% vs. 12.17%) and 3× on SmolLM2 (5.16% vs. 15.61%) while lifting utilization entropy to 0.901-0.915. Honestly reporting the full regime map: at ultra-sparse k = 16, fixed per-sample TopK+AuxK retains the lowest absolute FVU (0.3157 Qwen3 / 0.3038 SmolLM2) and BatchTopK+AuxK revives slightly more deeply-dead features than pure-MSE MarginProb (51.0% vs. 58.5% on Qwen3), because encoder-only margin gradients primarily rescue near-boundary latents whereas AuxK also updates Wdec on deeply-dead atoms. Combining both (MarginProb+AuxK) outperforms BatchTopK+AuxK on dead latents overall (Wilcoxon p = 0.181) and dominates every metric at k = 32.

Cross-Seed Feature Consistency Trade-Off (Honest Negative/Mixed Finding). Evaluating 1-to-1 Hungarian bipartite matching across 3 independent random seeds reveals a subtle capacity effect: methods with 75-90% dead latents (JumpReLU, BatchTopK-STE at k=16) exhibit artificially higher Hungarian cosine (0.32-0.41) simply because their tiny alive subsets share only the highest-variance top principal directions, whereas high-utilization dictionaries (MarginProb, BatchTopK+AuxK, TopK+AuxK) populate many finer-grained directions with lower pairwise overlap (0.16-0.26). We therefore caution against interpreting raw cross-seed cosine without controlling for active dictionary size.

Live Transformer Causal Splice & 8× Overcomplete Scaling (Tables 1 & 4, Fig. 1b,c,e). Splicing k = 32 SAEs directly back into the live transformer residual streams confirms that reconstruction FVU gains translate directly into causal language-modeling fidelity: on Qwen3-1.7B L13, MarginProb-BatchTopK recovers 98.6% of cross-entropy loss (Logit KL = 0.2910) vs. 97.7% (KL = 0.3523) for ungated v1 and 98.9% for BatchTopK-STE; on SmolLM2-1.7B L12, MarginProb recovers 99.4% (KL = 0.2040). 6. Ablation Studies

Table 3 and Fig. 1d sweep boundary surrogate weight λ ∈ {0.00, 0.05, 0.15, 0.30, 0.60} and scale-invariant temperature τrel ∈ {0.15, 0.35, 0.70} on Qwen3-1.7B L13 (k = 32, dsae = 8,192, 3 seeds). The λ sweep exhibits a striking U-shaped FVU curve coupled with monotonic dead-latent elimination: moving from λ = 0.00 (exact BatchTopK-STE, FVU = 0.2644, dead = 54.9%) to λ = 0.05 immediately drops FVU to its optimum 0.2608 while cutting dead latents by -22.4 pp to 31.9%. At λ = 0.30, dead latents plunge 6× to 9.0% while FVU (0.2616) STILL beats BatchTopK-STE; at λ = 0.60, dead latents vanish to 1.18% (42× reduction, entropy 0.914) with FVU (0.2663) still vastly better than ungated v1 (0.2762).

7. Honest Limitations & Future Work

  • Decoder Resurrection Gap: Because the hard forward pass zeroes inactive activations, MarginProb routes smooth gradients exclusively into Wenc and benc, not Wdec. Deeply dead features (far below τ) therefore still benefit from AuxK.
  • Training Horizon & Dictionary Width: We scale 4× beyond v1 (dsae = 8,192 over 336,474 total leak-free tokens across two models), which is still smaller than production 65k-131k SAEs trained on 100M+ tokens; long-horizon dead-latent dynamics at 16×-64× expansion merit future verification.
  • Automated Feature Interpretability: We evaluate downstream semantic preservation via an 8-class research-category linear probe rather than LLM-as-a-judge auto-interpretability scores on individual latents.
