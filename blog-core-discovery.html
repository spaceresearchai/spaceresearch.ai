CoreRank Discovery: Mining Surprising, Non-Common-Knowledge Findings Across 1,715 AI Papers

Vector search finds obvious neighbors; citation sorting amplifies consensus. We tested whether Algorithmic Core Extraction (ACE) and Early-Exit CoreRank can invert retrieval, scanning 1,715 papers in 15.6 seconds on a single RTX 5090 to surface counterintuitive results, cross-field mechanism bridges, and hidden empirical contradictions.

ABSTRACT

Can a linear Stage-1 activation core find genuinely surprising science that is not common knowledge? Our empirical V-dip audit on Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B first proves why the naive approach fails: a direct 1D surprise_core calibrated on hype-free claims achieves only ρ = 0.098 because surprise is relational (a delta against a prior), not an intrinsic scalar property of a single abstract. In contrast, fluff-invariant rigor_core forms a verified Deep Conceptual Bottleneck (ρ = 0.951, cross-lexicon Flip AUC plunging to 0.309 then rebounding +0.214), alongside strong hype_core (ρ = 0.772) and prior_plausibility (ρ = 0.515) axes. By combining these into four relational CoreRank discovery mechanisms, (1) Prior-vs-Evidence Bayesian Surprise, (2) 8-Axis Topic-Canceled Cross-Domain Mechanism Bridges, (3) De-Biased Stage-2 Keep-1D Dense Contradictions (AUC = 0.940, −53.5% order bias), and (4) Citation-Residual & Off-Core Causal Outliers (slashing tournament Condorcet cycles from 96.3% → 29.8%), and independently cross-validating with pruned Qwen3-Reranker-4B (L22), we achieve a +2.05 4B surprise lift over random baselines, a +1.70 nat/tok prior surprisal gap over top-cited controls, and surface live zero-citation discoveries now searchable in our CoreRank Discovery tab.

1. The epistemic trap: why naive "surprise" probes fail

Standard scientific search optimizes for familiarity: BGE cosine similarity pulls papers using the same vocabulary inside the same subfield, and citation counts reward ideas that have already hardened into consensus. Finding something genuinely surprising requires the exact opposite geometry.

Before trusting any discovery score, we ran our V-Dip Conceptual vs. Lexical Bottleneck Auditor (corerank/auditor.py) across five distinct comparative lexicons (surprising/obvious, unexpected/predictable, counterintuitive/conventional, unusual/standard, and a Yes/No control) on Qwen/Qwen3-1.7B:

  • Naive 1D surprise_core fails cleanly: When calibrated on strictly neutral, hype-free technical claims, rank-1 ACE yielded only train ρ = 0.098 and a weak partial V-dip (rebound +0.052). Worse, raw LLM pairwise prompting on our adversarial hype suite (plain counterintuitive findings vs. buzzword-inflated textbook truisms) suffered 100% positional flip bias.
  • rigor_core and prior_plausibility succeed cleanly: Calibrated against adversarial buzzword fluff, Stage-1 rigor_core at layer L13/28 achieved train ρ = 0.951 and triggered a textbook Deep Conceptual Bottleneck (cross-lexicon Flip AUC plunging to 0.309, then rebounding +0.214 at L28). Across all 1,715 papers, Corr(z_rigor, z_hype) = −0.899, while stripping rhetorical cue words broke the buzzword shortcut (Corr(z_surprise_clean, raw_hype_cue) = +0.092).

2. Method 1: Prior-vs-Evidence Bayesian Surprise

Instead of asking the model whether an abstract "sounds surprising," we treat the backbone LLM's pretrained weights as a proxy for common knowledge. For every paper we extract the core empirical claim sentence with hype vocabulary stripped (surprisingly, novel, breakthrough, contrary to, etc.), then run a single O(N) early-exit multi-rubric pass: 

M1(i) = −z_prior(clean_claim_i) + 0.8 · z_surprise(clean_claim_i) + 0.9 · z_rigor(paper_i) − 0.6 · z_hype(paper_i)

Common knowledge scores high on z_prior and gets suppressed; unbacked hype scores low on z_rigor and high on z_hype and gets rejected. Independently scored by pruned Qwen3-Reranker-4B (L22), the top-20 surfaced papers jumped from −1.54 to +0.51 on 4B surprise (+2.05 lift) with 0.97+ mean claim groundedness. Highlights buried at 0-7 citations:

  • Steerable but Not Decodable: Function Vectors Operate Beyond the Logit Lens (7 cites, 4B surprise +9.30, rigor 7.51): Proves across 12 tasks and 6 models that Function Vectors routinely steer outputs accurately even where neither the logit lens nor a tuned lens can decode the answer at any layer, directly mirroring CoreRank's own Stage-1 vs. unembedding separation.
  • FlagRot: Selective Rotation for Low-Bit KV Caches (0 cites, 4B surprise +9.16, rigor 7.04): Refutes the standard assumption that Hadamard/orthogonal rotation always helps low-bit KV quantization, rotation flattens spiky slices while creating new peaks in flat slices inside the same key vector.
  • The KL Divergence Is Never Negative, Except in PyTorch (0 cites, 4B surprise +7.65): Shows across 8.5M certified inputs that clamping negative F.kl_div values hides silent gradient corruption: over 85% of FP32 values have ≥1% relative error near 10^-7, and even promoting one precision leaves 7-51% wrong across 22 of 23 configs.
  • Sparse Autoencoder Attribution Graphs Are Decided at the Margin (0 cites, #1 on 1.7B, 4B rigor 7.44): Zeroing a single TopK SAE feature swaps the next layer's active set in 74%-89% of cases; modeling margin swaps cuts error by 84%, and exact recomputation of 48 interventions runs 3.5× faster than the attribution graph itself.
  • Attention Temperature Confounds Zero-Shot Context Extension (0 cites, lowest hype in the corpus at z = −3.84): Exposes how pure-BF16 AdamW updates on a 1.0-initialized gain fall below half a BF16 ULP, leaving gains frozen across 22 checkpoints and inflating apparent sparse-attention context-extension wins by 37-58%.

3. Method 2: Cross-Domain Mechanism Bridges

To discover Swanson-style undiscovered public knowledge across subfields, we calibrated eight topic-canceled Stage-1 mechanism cores (low_rank_spectral, sparse_gating, heavy_tail_outliers, depth_early_exit, causal_intervention, iterative_recurrence, memory_eviction_tiling, and symmetry_debiasing), scored all 1,715 papers in 3.3 seconds, projected out all 384 BGE topic dimensions via ridge regression, and mined cross-category pairs in the bottom 25% of BGE cosine similarity (< 0.71) with high ACE mechanism alignment (> 0.80).

Our standout actionable bridge, invisible to standard cosine retrieval (BGE = 0.706) yet scoring ACE_mech = 0.879 and 4B Transfer = +12.31 (vs. 7.97 random), linked ProbMoE: Differentiable Probabilistic Routing for Mixture-of-Experts (architectures) with BatchTopK Sparse Autoencoders (mech-interp-saes). Both tackle non-differentiable discrete TopK subset selection from opposite ends, immediately motivating a concrete GPU experiment: importing ProbMoE's exact marginal-probability surrogate gradient into BatchTopK SAE training.

5. Honest limits & closing the autonomous research loop

Testing all four methods live also exposed three real limitations that we explicitly address in our engineering pipeline:

  1. CoreRank is a high-speed discovery funnel, not a truth oracle: A 1.7B-4B early-exit pass identifies claims that contradict consensus while reading with concrete mechanistic rigor, but cannot verify mathematical proofs or catch subtle experimental bugs on its own. Every top candidate must pass into actual code execution on real silicon.
  2. Non-linear citation baselines matter: Fitting a naive linear log(citations) ~ year model over-predicted citations for 2019 classics like Multi-Query Attention; capping pre-2026 sleeping beauties at < 50 citations isolates truly overlooked work cleanly.
  3. Off-core energy detects both paradigm-breakers and OOD noise: High energy in orthogonal ACE directions Q[2:8] surfaced unconventional architectures (such as continuous-time wave neurons and 2-bit SRFT looped MoE) alongside out-of-domain pure-math arXiv entries, and large Stage-1→Stage-2 rank drops cleanly separated the two.

To close the loop from discovery → experimental verification → publication, we built the Space AI Autonomous Research Engine (scripts/research_engine/), which continuously mines our vector DB via CoreRank, checks live GPU VRAM safety headroom, runs bounded PyTorch experiments on our RTX 5090, compiles full technical papers, and publishes timestamped research notes directly into our day-grouped blog feed.
